From Mariel — The Founding Story

I was drowning.

For eight years I co-ran iSLAS Filipino BBQ & Bar in Toronto with my husband Marc — starting at 1690 Queen Street West in December 2017, then opening a second location at 890 Wilson Ave in December 2024. Two locations. 20+ staff. Mid-to-high six-figure revenue. We survived COVID as a two-person team and earned recognition from BlogTO, Foodism, Toronto Life, and the Philippine Consulate. We hosted three annual Night Market events starting in summer 2022, each with 30–60 vendors.

And every day, I was drowning.

"At 11 PM, after 16 hours on my feet, I sat with seven spreadsheets open trying to reconcile what the POS said, what the bank said, what Uber said, what the cash drawer said — and I could not answer the simplest question: did we make profit today?"

Eight different platforms. POS, delivery apps, email, spreadsheets, accounting software, bank apps, calendars, supplier portals. None of them talked to each other. Permits arrived as surprises. Maintenance broke before warnings reached me. People said: "You should delegate." But hiring a manager costs $60K. A bookkeeper, $2–5K a month. For a small operator, that is not delegation. That is bankruptcy.

I have a finance background — SAP FI/CO at Accenture, accounting at Daniels, finance at Scotiabank. And I was still drowning. Which means the problem is not operator incompetence. The problem is systemic fragmentation. Capital does not fix this. Foundation does.

In late 2025 we paused operations. I traveled through the Philippines and Japan to reflect. I enrolled in the Rotman Executive Program in Generative & Agentic AI for Business. I incorporated Islas Tech (legally: 1001561700 Ontario Inc.) to formalize this work. The next business I am building is the system that fixes the operating dilemma I lived.

The Operator's Reality

Drowning in fragmentation.

If you operate a small business doing $300K–$5M in revenue in Canada, you are managing this every day:

i.

Eight different platforms

POS, delivery apps, email, spreadsheets, accounting software, bank apps, calendars, supplier portals — none of them talk to each other.

ii.

No real-time visibility

You don't know your true cost until midnight in a spreadsheet, after a 16-hour day. Decisions made from anxiety, not clarity.

iii.

Surprise deadlines

Permits, renewals, maintenance, filings — always a surprise, always a fine, always one more thing you couldn't see coming.

iv.

Trapped in operations

16-hour days managing systems instead of growing the business. Innovation, scaling, and customers all wait.

v.

Can't afford to delegate

A CFO costs more than your net profit. A controller is out of reach. So you do it all yourself — and slowly burn out.

vi.

Capital can't fix it

Funding compounds fragmentation. The operators who fail with capital are the ones who scale revenue without scaling foundation.
